Events Team

The international collective Philosophy, Critique, and 'AI' (PCAI) consists of early career researchers working on questions regarding ‘Artificial Intelligence’ (AI) from a predominantly philosophical perspective.

The PCAI group seeks to foster space for politicised philosophical reflection on ‘AI’, while refusing to succumb to overly simplistic characterisations of either ‘salvation’ or ‘demise’.

The collective understands techno-scientific practices to be situated within social structures that are in turn embedded in logics of domination. As such, we recognise that political reflections on ‘AI’ should be approached through a plurality of different critical theoretical lenses (e.g., sociopolitical, epistemic, normative), which pertain to minoritised (e.g., feminist, decolonial, queer) perspectives.

Presentation 6: 11.05.2026; 16h-17h CET – dr. Sebastián Lehuedé, King’s College London (online)

Sustainable to Whom? In-Situ AI and Peasant Struggles in Colombia

Many 'AI for sustainability' initiatives pursue ambitious goals such as emissions reduction, yet local impacts and alternative approaches have been largely overlooked. Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork in Colombia, this talk analyses two initiatives: one by Microsoft in the Amazon and another by peasant communities in the High Andes. Through this analysis, it traces the emergence of 'in-situ AI', highlighting its capacity to engage with local ecological struggles and address environmental injustices. Nevertheless, questions remain about the capacity of in-situ AI to address urgent, planetary-scale environmental challenges.

More information about the speaker: https://slehuede.me

Work in Progress 5: 15.04.2026; 16h-18h CET – Eloise Soulier (online)

Cyborgs, care robots, robodogs: is the idea of relations to machines feminist? 

Feminist philosophy in general has paid particular attention to relationality, as a reaction to the Western Enlightenment ideal of atomistic rational selves that has served to exclude many, including women. For some feminists, this humanist ideal places women on the side of the non-humans and grounds the need for a posthuman philosophy of interconnected and interrelated beings. But in a world where machines are so deeply intertwined with capitalist exploitation and masculinist ideologies, is it furthering feminist purposes to consider that we have relations to machines?

Eloise Soulier is a PhD candidate and research associate in the Ethics in IT Research Group at the University of Hamburg, chaired by Professor Judith Simon. Her dissertation work explores relational conceptions of autonomy and what these can contribute to the discourse on human autonomy in the context of digital technologies, drawing from feminist moral philosophy. More broadly, drawing from feminist epistemology and feminist philosophy of language, she researches the ways in which our conceptual choices shape the – digital - realities we live in, and whether and how we can act on it.

Reading Session 6: 18.06.2025; 17h-19h CET – ‘What’s ‘Good’ in ‘Good AI’?

To wrap up the academic year, we organise a final reading and discussion session on the question ‘What’s ‘Good’ in ‘Good AI’? We will be discussing a chapter from Simon Lindgren’s Critical Theory of AI (2023) and an article written by Mona Simion entitled "Two Dilemmas for Value-Sensitive Technological Design" (2024) in the Feminist Philosophy and Emerging Technologies handbook. The aim is to (notably) discuss our preconceptions of what a 'good', less harmful or evaluative AI could be/is thought to be in our current neoliberal economy.

Paper Presentation 4: 09.04.2025; 17h-19h CET – Asher Kessler (online)

On the 9th of April, we are excited to welcome Asher Kessler, who will present his latest paper "Longtermism, Big Tech, and the Rebalancing of Historical Time: A Benjaminian Critique", in which he discusses the perception and valuation of time in the big tech industry in the Silicon Valley.

Asher is currently finishing his PhD at LSE in the Media and Communications department. His research focuses on Facebook/Meta, where he examines how high-level actors in and around the company came to think and speak about time, space, and their ability to change the world around them. He analyses how actors in and around Facebook/Meta utilise the language and times of progress and exponentiality to produce a sense of historical time which is preoccupied with the future.

For more information about his research, you can consult his website: https://asherkessler.com/

There will be a 20-minute presentation followed by a Q&A and discussion.
